Unit 2: World of Color
WEEK 2
Science and Engineering: Class Soup
Children cook real soup together.
Big Ideas Color is a property of many natural and human-made things.
There is an infinite variety of colors.
Guiding
Question
What colors do we notice all around us?
How do colors provide information?
Family
Engagement
Invite families and caregivers to share their favorite soup. This could be a
recipe, or photograph, an herb packet, or even donated empty food
containers (e.g., an empty box of noodles).
If interested, invite family members to share their expertise by coming in to
talk about the food or demonstrating how to cook the food.
Invite caregivers to come into cook soup together with the children.
Vocabulary gather: to collect things
ingredients: things that are mixed together to make a dish
mix: put different things together
prepare: to make something
recipe: a list of ingredients and directions for making food
stir: to mix by moving a spoon/ladle in a circle
Materials ● simple recipe
Select a soup recipe that children can easily follow. There is an
example in the back of Soup Day or invite families to share a
favorite recipe.
● chart paper and markers
Transfer the recipe onto chart paper. Where possible make the
recipe visual (e.g., next to the “6 cups of broth”, draw out 6 cups).
● ingredients to make the soup
To make the cooking process easier, prepare some of the
ingredients ahead of time. And leave some of the preparation for
the children (e.g, children can help wash and cut vegetables)
● cooking implements such as a large pot, colanders, child-safe

Intro to Centers In the story Green is a Chile Pepper/Red is a Dragon we noticed the
many colors of different foods. What foods are special to your
family?
Invite 1-2 responses.
In Soup Day, the child and mom worked together to cook soup. And
you are making soup in Dramatic Play! A soup is made by cooking
ingredients slowly in a big pot. In Soup Day, they used ingredients
such as carrots, celery, and mushrooms. So many different colors!
Today we will cook a real soup together! Let’s look at the recipe.
This is the list of food that we will need and also the directions.
Show recipe on chart paper. Point to the sections of the recipe while talking
through it.
I already gathered the ingredients, or the foods that we will need to
make the soup. Let’s look at the ingredient list: _____ [list
ingredients]. First we will wash the ingredients and chop them into
small pieces. Then we will stir them all together in our pot and add
seasonings, like salt, pepper, and herbs, for extra flavor. We will
make everything very hot so that it cooks all the ingredients. How
might our soup change after we add heat to it?
Harvest responses from children.
When we’re finished, we can taste the soup we cooked together
today during lunch.
During Centers Children first wash their hands and cook soup together. As small groups join
the Science and Engineering Center, share what step of the recipe is being
followed. Use ordinal language (first, second, third) while following the
recipe. Invite children to take turns completing steps of the recipe (e.g.,
First Bryce will pour in the milk; second, Camilla will add the flour; third
Yoshi will stir the ingredients with a spoon.).
Encourage children to notice the characteristics of the ingredients, such as
the color, texture, smell or amount before cooking. Then ask how they
think the ingredients might change when we add heat to them

Differentiation
Ideas
● Use the visual soup recipe connected to Soup Day (see Dramatic
Play, U2 W1 ) to support children with making a soup.
● Use the cooking topic board (see Dramatic Play, U1 W1) to support
children with communication as they prepare a soup with real
ingredients.
● Break down the experience over several days, one day each for:
exploring the vegetables textures and names, washing the
vegetables, cutting the vegetables/making observations about the
insides, mixing the vegetables and cooking the soup.
○ Use the steps that are appropriate for your classroom.
● Partially cook vegetables (e.g., in a microwave) to make them easier
to cut.
● Ensure children know the difference between pretend and real
ingredients. Practice with the pretend ingredients before using the
real ingredients.
Facilitation ● How are our ingredients similar to or different from the ones in the
book, _____(Soup Day or Green is a Chile Pepper or Red is a
Dragon)?
● Which step in the recipe comes first/next? How do you know?
● What foods are special to you and your family?
● Have you eaten soup with your family before? What kind was it?
● What do we need to do first (second, third) to prepare our soup?
● What colors do you see in this soup?
● Why do you like this soup?
● How does the soup change when we add heat to it?
Standards PreK-LS1-3(MA). Use their five senses in their exploration and play to
gather information.
PreK-PS1-4 (MA) Recognize through investigation that physical objects and
materials can change under different circumstances.
