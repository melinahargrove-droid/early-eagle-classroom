READ ALOUD

Familiar Books From Home

  • Select one or two favorite books contributed by families, books about children’s interests, or a familiar classroom book if none were sent.
  • Introduce whose favorite book it is, the title, author, and illustrator. Invite the child to tell the group a little about the book.
  • Activate background knowledge: connect the story to children’s experiences and give them something to listen/read to find out.
  • Read the full text fluently. Pause 2–3 times to define selected vocabulary, ask what is happening, or use illustrations to think about a character’s feelings.
  • After reading, ask 1–2 questions such as: Why do you like this book? Why is it important to you or your family? What is your favorite part?
  • Thank the child/family and place the book in Library & Listening to revisit alone or with a friend.

DRAMATIC PLAY

Cooking

  • Introduce Dramatic Play as a place to pretend to be anyone or anything. Explain that it will change during the year and is currently set up like a kitchen.
  • Tour the kitchen furniture, appliances, food, and tools. Explain that kitchens are places where people prepare food.
  • Model pretending to be a family member making soup. Think aloud about what to cook, what ingredients are needed, and checking a recipe.
  • Model the sequence with first/next/then/finally: wash, chop, cook, and mix; then serve the pretend food.
  • Model cleanup by using labels to return food and tools to the correct bins/shelves.

ART STUDIO

Paper Collages

  • Introduce Art Studio as a place to explore materials and share ideas. Connect to the art-making shown in Lola Goes to School page 19.
  • Define collage: art made by gluing or pasting different pieces to make a picture. Show paper pieces and collage examples.
  • Model deciding which piece to use. Explain decide means make a choice.
  • Model a glue stick: cap off, twist up only a little, gently rub glue on the paper, and adhere the piece—stick it on.
  • Recap the glue so it does not dry out, put the collage on the drying rack, and choose another place to play.
  • Children may create any design they like and may work with a friend.
